# Joao Crus BJJ Kids AI-Spokesperson Ad Framework

**Status:** Working concept framework. Not approved for production, Meta upload, or activation.  
**Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Scope:** Concept, message, spokesperson, and testing architecture only. Do not call Seedance, ElevenLabs, image-generation, or Meta APIs.  
**Primary outcome:** Qualified parent leads who speak with Joao and progress toward a free studio visit.  

## 1. Recommendation

Build the creative system around two child-stage personas and three genuinely different message angles. Generate two hook executions per angle, creating 12 master concepts before location localization.

Do not launch all 12 at once on the existing local-service budget. Use the matrix as a production library, then select 3–5 genuinely distinct concepts for each geographic wave.

| Market | Eligible child ages | Confirmed class | Correct message |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Dripping Springs | 5–7 | Little Champions, Mon/Wed 5:00–5:45 p.m. | Age-appropriate first step, purposeful practice, parent reassurance |
| Dripping Springs | 8–12 | Youth, Mon/Wed 5:50–6:35 p.m. | Technique, partner responsibility, problem-solving, after-school fit |

**Hard rule:** Do not advertise ages 5–7 in Austin until an Austin class for that age group is confirmed.

### Campaign sequencing

At the previously approved approximate $35/day budget, run the markets sequentially rather than splitting the budget:

1. **Dripping Springs wave:** parents of children ages 5–12, one broad local ad set, 3–5 concepts, Program Finder destination.
2. **Austin wave:** parents of children ages 8–12, one broad local ad set around Castle Hill, 3–5 concepts, Austin destination.

If both markets must run simultaneously, give each market an independently approved budget and keep geography, landing page, ad naming, and reporting separate. Do not let campaign allocation masquerade as a clean market test.

## 2. Spokesperson personas

These are representative adult guides, not customers or testimonials. Casting can vary, but the message must remain grounded in the child stage and local program.

### Persona P1: The First-Step Parent

- **Child stage:** 5–7
- **Market:** Dripping Springs only
- **Parent mindset:** Wants a structured first activity, but does not expect the child to arrive focused, coordinated, or fearless.
- **Primary tension:** “Will my child know what to do, and will the class meet them at their level?”
- **Desired future:** The child listens for one cue, communicates a boundary, tries, resets, and gradually participates with more confidence.
- **Spokesperson feel:** Warm, calm, reassuring, everyday parent energy. Home entryway, parked car, or neutral family setting.
- **Voice direction:** Conversational and measured. Never urgent or fear-based.
- **Safe phrasing:** “For parents looking for an age-appropriate first step…”
- **Avoid:** “My child trains here,” “Joao changed my child,” or any invented first-person result.

### Persona P2: The After-School Fit Parent

- **Child stage:** 8–12
- **Markets:** Dripping Springs and Austin
- **Parent mindset:** Wants more than entertainment or energy-burning. Values challenge, responsibility, technique, and a schedule that fits family life.
- **Primary tension:** “Will this hold their attention and give them something useful to work toward?”
- **Desired future:** The child encounters a problem, listens to coaching, makes an adjustment, and sees progress through practice.
- **Spokesperson feel:** Practical, direct, organized parent energy. Car pickup line, kitchen counter, or work-to-activity transition.
- **Voice direction:** Clear and confident, with slightly faster pacing than P1.
- **Safe phrasing:** “If you are comparing after-school activities…”
- **Avoid:** Claims that the viewer’s child is distracted, anxious, bullied, unsafe, or lacking confidence.

## 3. Message angles

### Angle A: Confidence Is Practiced

- **Category:** Desired outcome
- **Core belief:** Confidence does not need to exist before the first class. It can grow through listening, trying, adjusting, and recognizing progress.
- **Visible mechanism:** A missed attempt, Joao’s correction, a reset, and a more successful second attempt.
- **Proof asset:** Real, permission-cleared class or coaching footage.
- **Not a claim:** Do not promise a personality transformation or guaranteed confidence.

### Angle B: Tap, Stop, Reset

- **Category:** Safety and boundaries mechanism
- **Core belief:** One of the first useful lessons is how to communicate “stop” and respond when a partner communicates it.
- **Visible mechanism:** Tap, immediate release, respectful reset, coach guidance.
- **Proof asset:** A slow, clearly visible tapping sequence with Joao or another approved coach present.
- **Not a claim:** Do not say tapping eliminates injuries or guarantees off-mat behavior.

### Angle C: Every Repetition Has a Job

- **Category:** Teaching method and problem-solving
- **Core belief:** Kids are not simply burning energy. Activities are designed to practice body control, listening, cooperation, and physical problem-solving.
- **Visible mechanism by stage:**
  - Ages 5–7: short activity, one simple cue, movement, partner turn, reset.
  - Ages 8–12: technical problem, coached adjustment, partner responsibility, improved repetition.
- **Proof asset:** Real class sequence that visibly pays off the spoken explanation.
- **Not a claim:** Do not imply academic improvement, clinical treatment, or guaranteed behavior change.

## 4. Twelve master concepts

Each concept is a 15–20 second master. The location and schedule line is localized after the core script. The spokesperson is an adult guide, with real academy footage used for the mechanism whenever permissions allow.

### P1: First-Step Parent, ages 5–7, Dripping Springs

#### P1-A1: Confidence Does Not Come First

- **Visual hook:** Adult spokesperson at the doorway with a child’s backpack or water bottle in frame. No child shown.
- **Opening:** “A child does not have to walk into their first jiu-jitsu class feeling fearless.”
- **Body:** “Confidence can start with one small win: listening for a cue, trying a movement, then trying it again.”
- **Close:** “See the Little Champions program for ages 5–7 in Dripping Springs.”
- **On-screen text:** `CONFIDENCE CAN BE PRACTICED.`

#### P1-A2: The First Class Is Not a Test

- **Visual hook:** Spokesperson calmly tying an adult-sized belt or packing a class bag. Do not imply it belongs to their child.
- **Opening:** “The first class is not a test of whether a five-year-old is already confident.”
- **Body:** “It is a chance to watch, listen, join at the right pace, and begin collecting small wins.”
- **Close:** “Find an age-appropriate starting point with Joao Crus BJJ.”
- **On-screen text:** `NO PERFECT FIRST CLASS REQUIRED.`

#### P1-B1: The First Lesson Is Not Winning

- **Visual hook:** Spokesperson points to the words `TAP MEANS STOP`, then cut to approved class footage.
- **Opening:** “One of the first useful lessons in jiu-jitsu is not how to win. It is how to say stop.”
- **Body:** “Kids practice tapping, stopping for a partner, resetting, and trying again with a coach.”
- **Close:** “Little Champions meets Mon/Wed in Dripping Springs.”
- **On-screen text:** `TAP. STOP. RESET.`

#### P1-B2: Two Sides of a Boundary

- **Visual hook:** Split text: `I CAN TAP` / `I STOP WHEN THEY TAP`.
- **Opening:** “Tapping teaches both sides of a boundary.”
- **Body:** “A child practices communicating stop, and a partner practices responding right away.”
- **Close:** “See how ages 5–7 begin at Joao Crus BJJ.”
- **On-screen text:** `A SHARED STOP SIGNAL.`

#### P1-C1: A 45-Minute Class Should Not Feel Like a Lecture

- **Visual hook:** Quick sequence of balance, crawl, partner turn, and reset.
- **Opening:** “A class for ages 5–7 should not feel like a long lecture.”
- **Body:** “Joao uses short activities, simple cues, movement, and repetition so young kids can keep learning.”
- **Close:** “Explore Little Champions in Dripping Springs.”
- **On-screen text:** `SHORT CUES. PURPOSEFUL PRACTICE.`

#### P1-C2: Every Game Has a Job

- **Visual hook:** Adult guide says the line, then the screen labels one observable activity and purpose.
- **Opening:** “In Little Champions, every game has a job.”
- **Body:** “A balance game practices body awareness. A partner game practices taking turns. A reset practices trying again.”
- **Close:** “Find the right first step for ages 5–7.”
- **On-screen text:** `EVERY GAME HAS A JOB.`

## 6. Production grammar for later AI testing

### Video structure

1. **0–2 seconds:** Parent-context visual and one clear hook.
2. **2–7 seconds:** Name the belief or tension without diagnosing the viewer’s child.
3. **7–14 seconds:** Show the real teaching mechanism through approved class footage.
4. **14–18 seconds:** Local program, age, schedule, and one CTA.

### Formats

- Produce a 9:16 master for Reels and Stories.
- Produce a 1:1 or 4:5 placement version for Feed.
- Keep captions burned in and readable without sound.
- Treat placement crops as versions of one concept, not additional test hypotheses.

### AI-spokesperson guardrails

- Use adults only. Do not generate AI children for Joao ads.
- The spokesperson is a guide or narrator, never a fabricated parent customer.
- Ban invented first-person experience: “My child goes here,” “Joao helped us,” “we enrolled,” or similar statements.
- Do not clone Joao, a parent, coach, or student’s voice without explicit permission.
- Pair the synthetic introduction with real academy mechanism footage whenever possible.
- Keep visual ethnicity, household type, and gender as casting diversity, not as claims about who has a problem or who will convert.
- Review final assets for required synthetic-media disclosures and current Meta policy before use.

## 7. Copy and claim rules

- Do not imply the viewer’s child lacks confidence, has behavioral problems, is bullied, is unsafe, or has a medical or emotional condition.
- Do not promise confidence, focus, safety, academic improvement, self-defense outcomes, or behavioral transformation.
- Use observable practice language: listen, tap, stop, reset, balance, take turns, adjust, solve, and try again.
- Do not publish price, trial deposit, uniform, savings, guarantee, or membership terms until explicitly approved.
- The accurate next step is: a parent answers practical questions, Joao personally calls, and they may arrange a free studio visit where the child can observe or participate.
- The quiz does not automatically book a class.
- Use no em dashes in public copy.

## 9. Measurement and decision rules

### Primary outcomes

- Qualified parent conversations
- Free studio visits scheduled
- Free studio visits attended
- Enrollments
- Qualified cost per lead by market, child stage, angle, and concept

### Diagnostic signals

- 3-second video hold
- 25% and 50% video completion
- Outbound click-through rate
- Landing-page conversion rate
- Cost per submitted lead
- Spend distribution and frequency

### Interpretation

- High CTR with low qualified-lead rate means the hook attracted attention but did not qualify the right parent.
- Low delivery does not prove a concept failed. Meta may concentrate spend before every concept receives a fair read.
- The initial ad set is a winner-selection tournament, not a clean causal test.
- Once an angle emerges, use Meta Creative Test or a controlled sequential test to compare two hooks while holding spokesperson, body, CTA, destination, market, and format constant.
- Graduate concepts based on qualified conversations, visits, and enrollments. Do not graduate from views or clicks alone.
