# Google Ads Keyword Playbook

**Project:** Joao Crus BJJ
**Status:** Saved for future use. Google Ads is deferred from the current launch and should not be activated until Diego explicitly reopens the channel.
**Source reviewed:** Jackson Blackledge's [Google Ads keyword research thread](https://x.com/blvckledge/status/2082761484556497402), published July 30, 2026

## Executive recommendation

Use the thread's intent-first research process, but adapt it for a low-volume local service business. Start with commercial and transactional searches, exact and phrase match, tightly themed landing pages, strong negatives, and conversion-quality feedback from HighLevel. Do not use the thread's ecommerce volume thresholds or broad-match scaling advice literally.

For Joao, the initial Google Search strategy should prioritize:

1. Kids and age 3-7 classes in Dripping Springs.
2. Adult beginner BJJ in Dripping Springs.
3. High-margin private instruction in Dripping Springs and Austin.
4. Brand protection for Joao Crus searches.

The Parent Guide should capture informational searches organically. Do not pay for those searches until the transactional campaigns prove profitable.

## What to retain from the thread

- Classify keywords by intent before looking at volume.
- Prioritize transactional and commercial searches first.
- Use Keyword Planner, forecasts, search-term reports, competitor tools, and live search suggestions together.
- Organize ad groups around one intent and one landing-page promise.
- Treat broad match as a later-stage discovery tool.
- Maintain a strong negative-keyword list.
- Review actual search terms continuously rather than treating research as a one-time task.

## What to change for Joao

### Do not require 30 or 1,000 monthly searches

A hyperlocal query can be valuable even when Keyword Planner reports very low volume. One enrolled family or one recurring private client may justify a keyword that receives only a few relevant searches per month.

### Do not split exact and phrase into separate ad groups initially

At Joao's likely search volume, splitting match types would fragment data. Keep exact and phrase versions of the same intent inside one tightly themed ad group. Separate them only if later search-term data shows a meaningful performance or budget-control reason.

### Do not start with broad match

Broad match should wait until the account has:

- reliable primary conversions;
- enhanced conversions for leads;
- captured Google click identifiers;
- offline quality stages from HighLevel;
- enough qualified-lead and enrollment data for bidding;
- a mature negative list.

### Do not send all traffic to the homepage

Match every search theme to its closest program/location page. The ad, search term, page headline, CTA, and follow-up should describe the same program.

## Conversion and attribution requirements

Google Ads should not launch until the website can distinguish leads from meaningful business outcomes.

### Primary optimization events

1. Qualified form submission.
2. Confirmed intro or consultation booking.
3. Qualified phone lead.
4. Attended first visit, imported as an offline conversion.
5. Enrolled student, imported as an offline conversion with value when approved.

### Observation events, not initial bidding goals

- Page view.
- Schedule view.
- CTA click without completed contact.
- Click-to-call without a connected/qualified call.
- Parent Guide article view.

### Required implementation

- Google Ads conversion tags through GTM.
- Consent-aware tag behavior.
- Enhanced conversions for leads where permitted.
- Preserve `gclid`, `gbraid`, `wbraid`, UTMs, landing page, and referrer through the Vercel form endpoint.
- Store source and click identifiers in HighLevel.
- Push qualified, booked, attended, and enrolled stages back to Google Ads.
- Keep brand and non-brand reporting separate.
- Compare Google Ads reporting with GA4, HighLevel, and Zen Planner outcomes.

## Bid and launch sequence

1. Finalize the offer, rate card, booking destination, and conversion definition.
2. Obtain Joao-owned Google Ads, GTM, GA4, HighLevel, and domain access.
3. Run the seed list through Keyword Planner using the real website and local geography.
4. Review forecasts by campaign, but do not discard low-volume local terms automatically.
5. Launch exact and phrase only with a controlled click-based strategy and CPC guardrails while conversion data is immature.
6. Review search terms frequently during the first two weeks and add negatives immediately.
7. Optimize toward qualified leads, bookings, attendance, and enrollment rather than raw form volume.
8. Move to conversion-based bidding only after conversion tracking and lead-quality feedback are trustworthy.
9. Test broad match only in a controlled experiment after the prerequisites above are met.
10. Review seasonality around back-to-school, summer camps, holidays, and adult cohort launch dates.

Start with Search rather than Performance Max. Search provides the query-level control needed to validate local intent, negative keywords, location quality, and program economics before giving Google a wider automation surface.

If the approved budget cannot support every campaign, use Keyword Planner forecasts and business value to stage the rollout rather than spreading a small budget thinly. Kids/age 3-7 and private instruction are the first candidates, followed by the adult cohort and Austin youth.

## Reporting scorecard

Report weekly by campaign and separate brand from non-brand:

- spend;
- impressions and search impression share;
- clicks and CPC;
- search terms and negative additions;
- form leads;
- qualified leads;
- booked visits;
- attended visits;
- enrollments;
- cost per qualified lead;
- cost per attended visit;
- customer acquisition cost;
- conversion lag;
- program and location mix.

The optimization goal is not the cheapest lead. It is the lowest sustainable cost per enrolled, retained student or profitable private client.
