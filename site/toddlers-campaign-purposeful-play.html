Built differently for age three

MOST SCHOOLS START AT 4.
WE START AT 3 ON PURPOSE.

Most schools begin accepting children at age 4. We start at age 3, and there is a reason.

Teaching a 3-year-old is a different skill than teaching a 6-year-old. It takes short activities, constant movement, clear simple words, and a coach who knows how to turn “listen and wait your turn” into a game. Our method is Montessori-informed and built over 25 years of working with the youngest kids on the mat.

At 3, your child is already forming habits around frustration, sharing, listening, and trying hard things. That's exactly what our classes work on. Jiu-jitsu is how we do it. Confidence is what they keep.

Play with a purpose

EVERY GAME
HAS A JOB.

Young children learn through movement, repetition, and play. Crawling, rolling, balancing, following cues, and working with a partner give them regular opportunities to practice body control, attention, patience, and cooperation.

01

Crawl. Roll. Balance.

Gives children practice with coordination, body awareness, balance, and moving confidently in different directions.

02

Watch. Listen. Move.

Simple cue-based games ask children to focus, remember one instruction, wait, and move at the right time.

03

Partner. Pause. Take Turns.

Working with another child creates practice with cooperation, sharing space, and respecting personal boundaries.

04

Try. Reset. Try Again.

Small challenges make room to practice patience, persistence, and recovering when something does not work the first time.

05

Plan. Move. Solve.

Simple physical problems ask children to change direction, choose a next move, and work through a challenge.

Why movement matters

Research on preschool physical-activity programs suggests that regular, mentally engaging movement can support attention, inhibitory control, motor development, and school readiness.[1][2][3]

What grows here

SMALL WINS.
BIG SKILLS.

We use movement and simple games to practice the abilities parents want to see beyond the mat. Progress looks like trying again, waiting one more turn, naming a feeling, or finding the courage to join.

01

Confidence

Not a participation trophy. A real memory of facing something new and discovering, “I can do this.”

02

Focus & discipline

Listen for one instruction, move with purpose, stop safely, and practice the rhythm again.

03

Social skills

Share space, take turns, set a safe boundary, respect a partner, and become part of the group.

WHAT YOUR TODDLER LEARNS.

Real skills they can practice on the mat and carry into preschool, sports, and home.

Listening and following directions. Coach gives one instruction at a time. Kids practice hearing it and doing it.
Taking turns and waiting. Built into every game. It transfers straight to preschool and home.
Naming big feelings. Frustrated, scared, excited. We give them the words while they feel it.
Body control. Balance, falling safely, and moving with purpose. It is the physical foundation for every sport later.
Safe boundaries. How to say “stop,” and how to respect it when someone else says it.
Earned confidence. Small wins, every class. Not trophies. Real “I did it” moments.
What a class looks like

A CLASS WITH
TODDLER RHYTHM.

Classes are 45 minutes, built from short bursts: a game, a skill, a game, a skill. Nobody stands in line waiting. Short explanations, active practice, and clear transitions keep the class achievable.

00–08

Arrive & move

Warm up through a simple game while children settle into the room.

08–18

Learn one thing

A single movement or boundary skill demonstrated in clear language.

18–35

Practice through play

Repeat the skill inside games that keep bodies and attention moving.

35–45

Finish proud

Regroup, celebrate effort, and leave with a win worth talking about.

Parent questions

ASK THE
REAL STUFF.

Three-year-olds are three-year-olds. You do not need to pretend otherwise before reaching out.

Is my child really old enough?

The class is intentionally built around short activities, games, movement, and repetition. We start at age 3 because the format meets children where they are.

What if they are shy or won't join immediately?

That is okay. A first visit can include watching, warming up slowly, and joining one piece at a time. We are looking for a safe first step, not a performance.

Do parents stay?

Yes. Parents watch from the side, so your child can see you and you can see how the class is taught.

What should they wear?

Comfortable clothes they can move in are fine for the first visit. We will explain uniform options if you decide to continue.
