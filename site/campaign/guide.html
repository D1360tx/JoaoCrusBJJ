Parent guide

EVERYTHING PARENTS NEED TO KNOW.

Straightforward answers to the questions parents ask most before a child's first Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu class.

Plan a first classExplore kids programs
Parents stay and watch
Published by Joao Crus BJJ | Teaching perspective: Joao Crus, head instructor | Updated July 29, 2026

Children's Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu is age-structured, movement-based, and parent-visible. Age-appropriate classes start as young as three and use games and partner work rather than adult competition. They give children structured opportunities to practice listening, cooperation, body awareness, and clear boundaries over time. This guide answers the questions parents ask most before a first class.

Parent FAQ

WHAT PARENTS
ASK MOST.

Joao Crus BJJ accepts children from age 3. Classes at that age focus on movement games, listening, partner cooperation, and body control rather than competitive sparring or adult technique.

No experience is needed. Beginners at every age are expected and welcomed.

Yes. Parents may stay and watch every class.

Comfortable athletic clothes work for a first visit. A gi (uniform) is not required for the initial class.

Shy or reluctant children are common at the first class. The class structure is designed for short, low-pressure bursts. Most children settle once they see peers doing the same activities. Parents may stay visible throughout.

A tap is a signal that communicates stop. Partners are trained to respond immediately. Children learn to both communicate their own limit and respect their partner's limit. Read What Tapping Teaches a Young Child for a full explanation.

About the instructor

WHO TEACHES
YOUR CHILD.

Joao Crus is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt with Carlson Gracie and Ricardo De La Riva lineage, more than 25 years of teaching experience, and an academy he founded in Dripping Springs in 2003.

His teaching approach is relational and developmental first. Classes for young children use games, short activities, clear language, and age-appropriate partner work designed to match where children actually are, not where adults might prefer them to be.
