Why we start at 3 when nobody else does

Most schools tell parents of 3-year-olds to come back next year. We don't, and there's a reason.

Teaching a 3-year-old is a different skill than teaching a 6-year-old. It takes short activities, constant movement, clear simple words, and a coach who knows how to turn "listen and wait your turn" into a game. Our method is Montessori-informed and built over 25 years of working with the youngest kids on the mat.

At 3, your child is already forming habits around frustration, sharing, listening, and trying hard things. That's exactly what our classes work on. Jiu-jitsu is how we do it. Confidence is what they keep.

What your toddler learns

  • Listening and following directions. Coach gives one instruction at a time. Kids practice hearing it and doing it.
  • Taking turns and waiting. Built into every game. It transfers straight to preschool and home.
  • Naming big feelings. Frustrated, scared, excited. We give them the words while they feel it.
  • Body control. Balance, falling safely, moving with purpose. The physical foundation for every sport later.
  • Safe boundaries. How to say "stop," and how to respect it when someone else says it.
  • Earned confidence. Small wins, every class. Not trophies. Real "I did it" moments.

What a class looks like

Classes are 45 minutes. That sounds long for a 3-year-old, but the class is built from short bursts: a game, a skill, a game, a skill. Nobody stands in line waiting.

Parents stay and watch. Your child can see you, and you can see exactly what they're learning.

First time on a mat? That's normal. Almost every toddler who starts with us has never done a sport before.

Questions parents ask

Is 3 really old enough?

For our classes, yes. The class is designed for this age: short activities, games, movement, repetition. We've been teaching 3-year-olds for years. If your child can walk, follow simple directions sometimes, and be away from your lap for a few minutes, they're ready to try.

My kid is shy / doesn't listen / won't sit still. Will this work?

Those are the kids this program helps most. Shy kids build confidence one small win at a time. Busy kids get a place where moving IS the activity. Nobody is expected to be good at listening on day one. That's what we're teaching.

Do they need to be potty trained?

Ideally yes. If you're mid-training, talk to us and we'll figure it out together.

Do I stay during class?

Yes. Parents watch from the side. Your child can always see you.

What do they wear the first time?

Comfortable clothes they can move in. No equipment needed for the free class. We handle uniforms after you enroll.
