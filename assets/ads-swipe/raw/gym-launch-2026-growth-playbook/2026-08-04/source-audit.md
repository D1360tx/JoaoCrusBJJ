## Lead-magnet funnel

The document is both a tactical training asset and a Gym Launch acquisition funnel.

- Pages 2–18 establish the owner pain and the five-gear model.
- Pages 19–132 teach offers, traffic, nurture, sales, and Month 1 monetization.
- Page 43 introduces the implementation/coaching bridge.
- Pages 133–135 present two paths: do it yourself slowly or implement with Gym Launch.
- Pages 43 and 135 contain a live link to `https://meetings.hubspot.com/gymlaunch/2026`.
- The linked HubSpot scheduler was live during capture and offered a **15-minute phone call** with Gym Launch.
- The PDF says qualified prospects may be offered either a flat-rate or performance-based service model. Exact service pricing is not disclosed.

The acquisition mechanism is: comprehensive educational asset → self-diagnosis across five gears → repeated implementation/accountability argument → short strategy call.

## Section map

### Pages 2–18: Why more leads are not enough

- Frames the gym as a money model rather than only a membership-count problem.
- Defines CAC, LTV, first-30-day cash, and client-financed acquisition.
- States the goal as Month 1 cash exceeding acquisition plus fulfillment cost.
- Introduces five gears: offers, traffic, lead nurture, sales, and Month 1 money.

### Pages 20–43: Gear 1, the Triple Offer System

1. **Transformation Offer:** a time-bound, outcome-led offer for cold paid traffic.
2. **Access Offer:** a lower-friction pass or first step for search, social, and warm researchers.
3. **Trigger Offer:** a seasonal or event-based reason to reactivate old leads and former members.

The document recommends 4–12 week fitness transformations and advertiser-stated floor pricing around $100/week for group training and $200/week for semi-private/personal training. Those are generalized Gym Launch recommendations, not Joao pricing evidence.

### Pages 45–69: Gear 2, traffic

- Paid creative strategy: creative volume plus variety.
- Recommends three-to-five program wrappers and ten-to-twenty ads per wrapper, creating a vault of 50+ ads.
- Organic framework: avatar × message × format.
- Organic message categories include struggle, shoutout, personal record/result, and tip.
- Organic formats include photo/caption, Reel, carousel, and screenshot/meme.
- Story framework: Hook → Story → Offer.
- Reactivation framework: one monthly “season or reason” offer and a three-text reply path.

### Pages 71–81: Gear 3, lead nurture

Four pillars:

1. Availability
2. Speed
3. Volume
4. Personalization

Recommendations include initial contact inside five minutes, three daily work blocks for the first five days, booking most consultations within 72 hours, a human confirmation call, and four-to-five reminders before the appointment. The playbook warns that generic automation and excessive bot reliance weaken response.

### Pages 83–117: Gear 4, sales

- Environment: lobby, greeting, proof, and dedicated consult space.
- Conversation: modified C-C-CLOSER framework.
- “Three Levels” model seeks a deeper emotional problem before presenting price.
- A/B Sale: transformation program versus ongoing membership.
- Diamond Sale: high-priced VIP anchor, two main options, then a fallback.
- Repeated focus on increasing close rate and cash collected on Day 1.

### Pages 119–132: Gear 5, Month 1 Money Model

Three planned touchpoints:

1. Day 1 signup and next-step scheduling
2. Week 1 nutrition/results meeting and potential product sale
3. Week 3 progress review, upgrade, pay-in-full, or referral conversation

The document argues that a choreographed first month can improve experience, retention, referrals, and cash flow. Its examples lean heavily on supplements, program upgrades, pay-in-full conversions, and referral monetization.

### Pages 133–135: implementation CTA

- Pick the weakest gear.
- Run the change for at least 100 leads.
- Or book a call for Gym Launch agency/coaching implementation.

## Advertiser-stated performance claims

The PDF makes several claims without supplying enough methodology in the document to independently validate them:

- Volume-and-variety ads reduced CPL by 30–70% in a roughly 50-gym beta.
- The same spend generated two-to-three times more leads.
- A Diamond Sale test increased close rates by roughly 25%.
- Average Day 1 collected reportedly increased from below $450 to $912 across participating gyms within two weeks.
- Some owners reportedly reached $200,000–$500,000 annual owner income.
- Ninety-four percent of appointments are reportedly scheduled during the first five days.
- The concluding Gym A/Gym B example presents approximately five times more members and twenty times more first-30-day cash.

No detailed sample selection, control design, confidence interval, spend distribution, market mix, offer mix, attribution method, retention adjustment, refund/chargeback adjustment, or full case-study ledger accompanies these claims. Treat them as lead-magnet proof claims, not forecast inputs for Joao.

## Mathematical and interpretation cautions

- The nurture example moves from five sales to eighteen sales and describes this as “almost 400% increase.” Eighteen is 3.6 times five, which is a 260% increase, not a 400% increase.
- Several examples mix paid leads, organic leads, reactivated leads, and referrals while describing the resulting blended CAC. The arithmetic can be internally consistent while masking different acquisition labor and channel costs.
- The final Gym A/Gym B scenario is hypothetical and changes lead volume, source mix, booking rate, close rate, and average first-month cash simultaneously. It illustrates compounding, not causal proof of any one tactic.
- “People who pay more get better results” is presented as a principle but is not established by the PDF as a universal causal relationship.

## Compliance and trust cautions

- Reactivation guidance mentions excluding opt-outs but does not adequately address consent provenance, TCPA risk, A2P registration, quiet hours, frequency disclosure, or state-level requirements.
- Three contacts per day for five days can be excessive for a local family audience.
- A scarcity message saying spots are “basically gone” and that the coach will “see what I can do” is acceptable only when capacity is genuinely nearly full.
- The Diamond model deliberately uses a premium anchor to change price perception and withholds a fallback until later. That can create mistrust if used as theater rather than transparent fit-based counseling.
- “Level 3” sales questions and phrases such as “fish in a barrel” or “savage closer” conflict with Joao's parent-safe, relationship-led positioning.
