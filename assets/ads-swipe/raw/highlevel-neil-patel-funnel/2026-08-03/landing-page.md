## Feature comparison graphic

The page displays these HighLevel capabilities:

- CRM and pipeline management
- Unlimited sales funnels
- Website builder
- Surveys and forms
- Email marketing
- Two-way SMS marketing
- Booking and appointments
- Workflow automations
- Courses and products
- Call tracking
- Reputation management
- Tracking and analytics
- Communities
- Document signing
- Gray-labeled mobile app

The graphic compares those categories with named competitor logos and advertiser-stated monthly values. It displays an **overall alternative-tool price of $1,612/month** and a HighLevel price of **$97**. The page does not label which HighLevel plan the $97 figure represents, and the visible hero/FAQ does not explicitly explain post-trial billing terms.

## FAQ

Only two questions were visible:

1. What is HighLevel?
2. What if I already have a HighLevel account?

The first answer describes HighLevel as an all-in-one marketing platform with automated texts, email campaigns, website builder, pipelines, and other tools. The second routes current users toward the Unlimited-plan affiliate-upgrade path.

No visible FAQ addressed:

- what happens after the trial;
- which plan the user enters;
- cancellation timing;
- usage charges for phone, SMS, email, AI, or other metered services;
- data migration;
- A2P registration;
- support or onboarding;
- attribution setup.

CTA:

No explicit SMS marketing consent language, automated-message disclosure, frequency statement, carrier-rate disclosure, or terms/privacy link appeared inside the captured modal. This does not prove later consent language is absent because Step 2 was not accessed.

## Ad-to-page message match

### Strong matches

- Ad teaches lead capture, CRM tagging, email/SMS follow-up, automation, booking, and buying.
- The feature graphic confirms that HighLevel provides those categories.
- “One platform” matches the ad's integrated-system promise.
- The paid URL preserves detailed campaign and creative identifiers while the modal opens in place.

### Gaps

- The ad is a highly specific implementation playbook; the page becomes a generic platform trial page.
- The page does not restate the five-to-seven-day sequence or show a ready-made workflow/template.
- It offers no case study or proof that this exact funnel improved customer acquisition.
- Neil's authority is used visually, but the page contains no substantive Neil quote, walkthrough, or bonus implementation asset.
- The fourteen-day trial is prominent; the visible post-trial plan and billing terms are not.
- The feature table's $97 price is image-based and lacks surrounding plan context.
