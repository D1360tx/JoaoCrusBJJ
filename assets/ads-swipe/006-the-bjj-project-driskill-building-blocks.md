## Executive read

This funnel sells **relief from complexity** rather than speed, rank, or a larger technique library. Its strongest move is absolving the prospect:

> **If it feels complicated, you're not bad at this. You're missing the building blocks.**

That line diagnoses the student's emotional pain and immediately supplies the product mechanism. The page then holds one coherent argument from hero through checkout: fewer principles, learned in order, outperform more disconnected moves.

The small Instagram overlay is also strategically interesting. It interrupts an unrelated Reel without requiring its own full-screen video. The advertiser borrows attention already earned by the background content, using only a logo tile, three cropped lines, and sponsored attribution.

## Ad-to-page message match

### Strong matches

- The visible overlay copy is almost identical to the hero deck.
- “Jiu-Jitsu Is Hard” is resolved by “Good Jiu-Jitsu Is Simple.”
- “Missing the building blocks” becomes the named product and curriculum structure.
- The page stays focused on overwhelmed white/blue belts rather than broadening to every audience.
- The $147 offer and lifetime-access terms appear near the first CTA and again at the close.

### Friction and leaks

- The overlay truncates “building blocks,” weakening comprehension for a cold viewer.
- No price, instructor, proof, lesson sample, or explicit CTA is visible in the captured placement.
- The supplied landing URL contains no incoming UTM parameters.
- Checkout CTAs hard-code `utm_campaign=organic`, which can obscure paid-source attribution.
- The Golden Goats free month becomes $97/month and is explained in the FAQ, not the primary offer summary.
- One 15+ lesson module is still being filmed.
- No visible guarantee or refund policy was observed.

## Why it may work

1. **Emotional absolution:** “You're not bad at this” reduces shame before selling.
2. **Single diagnosis:** confusion is framed as a missing foundation, not a memory or talent defect.
3. **Subtractive mechanism:** fewer principles is more distinctive than another large technique library.
4. **Authority fit:** Driskill's Rickson association supports a fundamentals-first story.
5. **Tangible demonstration:** the Earthquake escape turns an abstract principle into a visible lesson.
6. **Clean front-end economics:** one-time payment and lifetime access reduce subscription resistance.
7. **Long-form consistency:** every section returns to the same mechanism.
8. **Low-footprint placement:** the overlay can reach people inside unrelated Reels without producing a full ad video.

## Risks and weaknesses

- The overlay relies on another creator's context and provides very little room for proof or qualification.
- Very short testimonials show satisfaction but not meaningful outcomes.
- Repeated claims about safety and longevity are advertiser promises, not independently substantiated outcomes.
- The recurring Golden Goats trial can create trust friction if the $97/month continuation is not equally clear at checkout.
- Selling an unfinished module may create expectation-management issues.
- Preselected email marketing consent and fixed organic checkout attribution are implementation concerns.

## Joao adaptations

### 1. Parent-safe diagnosis for Little Champions

Use the same absolution structure without implying a defect in the child or parent:

> **If your three-year-old cannot stand in line through a long class, they're not bad at sports. They need a class designed for how young children learn.**

Mechanism:

> **Short activities. Clear cues. Purposeful play. Every game has a job.**

This fits Joao's age-3 differentiator and Montessori-informed teaching story better than a technique-course pitch.

### 2. Adult beginner retention

> **If Jiu-Jitsu feels like a pile of moves, you don't need to be more athletic. You need a foundation you can actually use.**

Joao can prove this through a short real-coaching clip, one principle, and the beginner-friendly Dripping Springs schedule.

### 3. Private Game Assessment

The diagnostic structure can become a premium private-coaching offer:

- identify the positions where the student feels lost;
- simplify the game into two or three priorities;
- deliver a written training roadmap;
- use follow-up private sessions to build and test the foundation.

### 4. Compact overlay test

A Joao overlay should sequence:

- audience identifier;
- compassionate diagnosis;
- one distinctive mechanism;
- Joao/academy identity;
- direct local CTA.

Example:

> **KIDS BJJ STARTING AT AGE 3**<br>
> If a typical class feels too long, your child isn't the problem.<br>
> See how Joao teaches through purposeful play.<br>
> **Dripping Springs · View the program**

## Do not copy

- Do not borrow Rickson/Driskill language or imply a relationship Joao does not have.
- Do not criticize local instructors or claim every other academy teaches random moves.
- Do not promise injury prevention, lifelong training, or guaranteed developmental outcomes.
- Do not hide recurring charges, use preselected marketing consent, or overwrite paid attribution with `organic` UTMs.
- Do not sell unfinished curriculum without explicit scope and delivery timing.

## Test ideas

| Test | Variable | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| Absolution vs. aspiration | “Your child isn't the problem” vs. “Start building confidence at age 3” | Qualified landing-page visits and accepted leads |
| Purpose vs. format | “Every Game Has a Job” vs. “45 minutes in short bursts” | Little Champions CTA-to-lead rate |
| Overlay vs. full-screen Reel | Compact placement over third-party Reels vs. Joao-led vertical video | Cost per qualified local visit and lead |
