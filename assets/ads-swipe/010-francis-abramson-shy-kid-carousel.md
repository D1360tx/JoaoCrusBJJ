## Creative anatomy

- **Format:** Seven-slide, silent-readable 4:5 carousel.
- **Visual hierarchy:** Blurred academy photography under large white text; yellow is reserved for timeline labels and one emphasis line; the final CTA uses a red ribbon.
- **Pattern interrupt:** The opening promises to show what a shy child looks like after six months rather than making a generic confidence claim.
- **Mechanism:** Specific behavioral markers progress from standing at the back to asking questions, helping newer students, self-correcting, making eye contact, and taking up space without apologizing.
- **Authority cue:** “I have watched this happen hundreds of times.”
- **CTA treatment:** Save and share for organic distribution; free-community bridge lives in the caption rather than the carousel.

## Why the structure is strong

- It turns “confidence” into observable parent-facing behavior.
- The timeline creates narrative momentum and makes every swipe answer “what happens next?”
- The transformation is framed as quiet presence, not becoming louder or aggressive.
- The final slide names jiu-jitsu as the mechanism only after the reader has seen the progression.

## Risks and weaknesses

- The six-month framing and broad behavioral progression are anecdotal, not a guaranteed outcome.
- “Afraid of everything and everyone” is vivid but can feel overly absolute or stigmatizing.
- The post is not locally qualified and does not bridge directly to an academy visit.
- The free-community destination is not visible in the post and remains unverified.

## Joao adaptation opportunity

Adapt the architecture, not the wording:

1. Parent-recognizable starting behavior.
2. First-class or first-week observation.
3. Early signs of participation and trust.
4. Specific coaching mechanism Joao uses.
5. A later observable milestone.
6. Parent reassurance that confidence can be quiet and earned.
7. Joao personally calls after the class finder to recommend a class and arrange a free studio visit.

Use verified Joao methods and real academy imagery. Avoid guaranteeing a six-month result or implying that all shy children follow the same timeline.
