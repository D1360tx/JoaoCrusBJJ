## Creative anatomy

- **Format:** 1290 × 2796 vertical Instagram Story capture.
- **Visual hierarchy:** Roger's face and body dominate the right side. The product name fills a high-contrast white block on the left. Yellow isolates the price. A white strip isolates the bonus. The CTA sits inside a large rounded white link sticker.
- **Color system:** Black and dark navy background, white product block, black headline, yellow price accent.
- **Authority cue:** The verified account and recognizable instructor are the creative. There is no need to explain credentials inside the ad.
- **Offer compression:** Product, price, bonus, and CTA are understandable in seconds.
- **Identity cue:** “White Belt” lets the viewer self-select immediately.
- **Pattern interrupt:** “JIU-JITSU IS EASY” creates tension with the beginner's expectation that it is confusing or difficult.

## Square video variant

### On-screen sequence

The 14.8-second square feed video compresses the offer into four beats:

1. **Product and price:** “The White Belt Toolkit” and “Just $49.99!”
2. **Problem-stage promise:** “The ultimate survival guide for beginners.”
3. **Authority mechanism:** “Progress faster with Roger as your mentor.”
4. **Offer stack:** “Free Nogi Essentials course worth $49.99!”

Roger's **“JIU-JITSU IS EASY”** shirt remains visible in the opening product card. Coaching and technique footage carry the rest of the video. There is no intelligible sales narration and no explicit in-video CTA in the captured source.

### Why it works

- **The offer is understood immediately.** Product identity and price appear before any explanation.
- **Authority is demonstrated.** Roger is shown teaching and physically demonstrating, not merely used as a portrait.
- **The benefit language is stage-specific.** “Survival guide for beginners” addresses the immediate white-belt experience.
- **The mechanism is personal.** The product is framed as Roger mentoring the buyer.
- **The bonus closes the sequence.** Ending on an equal-stated-value bonus maximizes perceived value.
- **It works without sound.** Every essential sales point is visible as text.

### Story versus square video

| Element | Vertical Story | Square video |
|---|---|---|
| Primary job | Fast offer recognition and click | Offer recognition plus authority demonstration |
| Movement | Static | Coaching and technique footage |
| Copy density | Product, price, bonus, CTA | Product, price, two benefits, bonus |
| Explicit CTA | “Claim this offer” sticker | None inside the source video |
| Authority | Portrait and verified account | Portrait plus real instructional footage |
| Best placement role | Story/Reels direct response | Feed prospecting or retargeting |

### Weaknesses

- Without the surrounding ad CTA, the video does not tell the viewer what to do next.
- “Progress faster” is broad and unsupported inside the 15-second creative.
- The video does not show lesson count, curriculum specificity, or a student result.
- The creative depends heavily on Roger's recognition and reputation.

### Direct Joao application

The useful pattern is a short, silent-readable sequence built from real class footage:

1. **Audience:** “BJJ built for ages 3–5.”
2. **Method:** “45 minutes. Short activities. Clear purpose.”
3. **Authority:** “A Montessori-informed method shaped by 25+ years of coaching.”
4. **Emotional outcome:** “Jiu-jitsu is how we do it. Confidence is what they keep.”
5. **CTA:** “Find their first class.”

Do not add an unconfirmed price, free trial, or savings claim. For Joao, the differentiator should be the age-specific teaching method and real academy experience rather than a discounted bonus stack.

## Landing-page flow

1. **Hero:** “The Ultimate Survival Guide for Beginners.”
2. **Emotional promise:** Gain confidence with Roger Gracie as mentor.
3. **Immediate price CTA:** Lifetime access for $49.99.
4. **Mechanism:** Simplified fundamentals, patient instruction, foundational principles, and a supportive learning path.
5. **Content preview:** Six example lessons followed by four benefit statements.
6. **Offer restatement:** One-time payment with no subscription required.
7. **Bonus:** Free Nogi Essentials course, stated value $49.99.
8. **Specificity:** A long technique list reduces ambiguity about what is included.
9. **Demonstration:** Technique video and another CTA.
10. **Proof:** John Danaher, George Burn, and Guy Ritchie testimonials.
11. **Convenience:** iOS, Android, Chromecast, and AirPlay access.
12. **Price anchoring:** $21.99 monthly, $189.99 annually, or the highlighted $49.99 one-time toolkit.
13. **Objection handling:** FAQ and checkout links.

## Ad-to-page message match

### Strong matches

- The same Roger portrait style and “JIU-JITSU IS EASY” shirt connect the ad to the hero.
- The $49.99 price is identical and repeated throughout the page.
- The beginner/white-belt audience remains consistent.
- The free Nogi Essentials bonus appears on the page with the same $49.99 stated value.
- Every major CTA preserves the Instagram campaign, content, and term UTMs into checkout.

### Friction or gaps

- The ad leads with the product name, but the hero leads with “Ultimate Survival Guide for Beginners.” “White Belt Toolkit” appears lower on the page.
- The page retains full-site navigation, Sign in, and Join links, which create exits from the direct-response path.
- The landing page is lengthy for a $49.99 purchase and repeats several ideas.
- The main proof is strong but appears relatively late.
- The three-option pricing section can shift attention from the advertised one-time offer to subscriptions.

## Why this offer is useful to study

1. **Identity-specific packaging:** It sells to “white belts,” not everyone who practices BJJ.
2. **Clear entry product:** One problem, one toolkit, one one-time price.
3. **Equal-value bonus:** The stated bonus value matches the core price, making the purchase feel like a two-for-one bundle.
4. **Authority as mechanism:** Access to Roger's judgment and structure matters as much as the lesson count.
5. **Specific curriculum:** The long technique list makes a digital course tangible.
6. **Low-friction economics:** Lifetime access avoids subscription resistance while the page also exposes buyers to the broader membership.
7. **Consistent attribution:** Instagram campaign parameters carry into the checkout URL.

## Adaptation opportunities for Joao

The useful lesson is the offer architecture, not the exact product or price.

### 1. Parent identity offer

- **Audience:** Parents of first-time children ages 3 to 7.
- **Working concept:** “The Little Champions First-Mat Toolkit.”
- **Promise:** Help a child arrive prepared, know what to expect, and build confidence through the first month.
- **Potential components:** First-class guide, parent observation checklist, one of Joao's children's stories, simple at-home listening/body-control games, and a progress conversation.
- **CTA:** Start the age-appropriate class-match or request a first-class plan.

### 2. Deposit-credit trial stack

- **Core offer:** A defined introductory class sequence, pending final price and operational confirmation.
- **Bonus:** Parent toolkit or storybook rather than an arbitrary discount.
- **Risk reversal:** Apply the deposit toward annual membership if the child continues, only after Joao confirms the mechanics.
- **Why it fits:** It preserves commitment while increasing perceived value without discounting the academy.

### 3. Authority-led creative

- Use Joao's real portrait as the dominant visual.
- Make one audience stage immediately obvious, such as “FIRST-TIME BJJ PARENTS” or “AGES 3–7.”
- Put the offer, bonus, and CTA on one frame.
- Let Joao's 25+ years, Montessori-informed method, and Carlson Gracie/De La Riva lineage provide the authority layer.

### Do not copy

- Do not use Roger's “White Belt Toolkit,” “Jiu-Jitsu Is Easy,” or black-belt-pathway language.
- Do not insert an unconfirmed price into Joao's trial offer.
- Do not promise a child outcome as guaranteed.
- Do not let a digital product distract from Joao's immediate local enrollment and private-coaching goals.
