## 1. THE MARKET BASELINE (reference data)

### Industry context
- Land-grab phase: IBJJF-registered academies grew 314 (2012) → 1,324 (2019) → ~7,500 (2025) → 8,928+ (2026). Demand rising, competition rising in every market.
- **Caution:** marketing amplifies everything about a gym, including weaknesses — operations (onboarding, retention, billing) must be ready before pouring money into acquisition.

### What the top models teach
| Model | Who | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Franchise machine** | Gracie Barra (~275 US locations, 1,000+ worldwide) | Edge isn't technique — it's **systemization**: standardized curriculum, certified instructors, automated billing/check-ins, branded gi/apparel revenue, $150–250/mo memberships |
| **Digital pioneer** | Gracie University (Rener/Ryron) | **Named, audience-specific programs** (Bullyproof, Women Empowered, Combatives) sell to people who would *never search "jiu jitsu"* — the best marketing innovation in BJJ |
| **Competition prestige** | Atos, Alliance, AOJ, MGA | Champions as marketing. But founder-centric brands carry **founder risk** (Atos mass exodus 2026 after Galvao allegations) |
| **Media-as-marketing** | Austin cluster: Kingsway/Danaher+Gordon Ryan, B Team, 10th Planet ATX, Atos ATX, Gracie Humaita ATX, 25+ gyms | Barely run local ads — weekly YouTube shows, viral personality content. Danaher: est. $20–25M over 8 yrs from instructionals |

### Pricing benchmarks
- **Group memberships:** national avg ~$145/mo unlimited adult; most academies $120–200; $120–300 by market/reputation.
- **Privates:** $75–150/hr (broader: $50–120 by belt/location); pack discounts standard. **Highest-margin revenue line.**
- **Drop-ins:** $15–30/class.
- **Kids:** $80–120/mo typical (2x/wk); family memberships save $30–50/mo and **drive stronger retention**.
- 🔑 **Underpricing kills gyms, not lack of demand.** Top 3 causes of academy failure: poor membership management, low retention, lack of structure. Price-competing attracts churners; **one-third of successful city studios charge $250+/member.**

### Revenue mix & margins
- **Kids programs commonly = 60–70% of gym revenue**, especially in the first 12–24 months; ages 7–12 are the largest martial-arts segment.
- Founder-coach models (owner teaches most classes): **50–60% net margin**; staffed models 25–35%.
- Industry median annual retention: **66.4%**. Failed payments alone = 2–3% involuntary churn; automated payment recovery cuts it 40–60%.
- Proven structures: tiered pricing by frequency, 6–12 mo contracts, cheap open-mat "gym access" tier (~half lowest package), Founder's Rate locking early members into 12-mo commitments.

### Marketing: what's working (2026)
1. **Referrals from existing students** — beats everything.
2. **Intro programs:** free first class = lowest barrier; **paid 2-week beginner program ($29–49) filters and often converts higher**; structured 4–6 session fundamentals on-ramps in groups of 4–8 at $49–99.
3. **Instagram culture content** — 63% of gym-goers discover gyms via social. Technique-video spam doesn't convert; **culture + beginner-visibility content does** (showing beginners addresses the intimidation barrier).
4. **Local Google search optimization** (GBP, reviews).
5. **Facebook still matters** for local-business research (71% of Americans use it); YouTube = hub-and-spoke SEO with 5–10 min anchor videos.
- Positioning angle: BJJ attracts problem-solvers — "human chess" (technique/strategy/intellectual challenge) outperforms intensity messaging. Most converting new students are men; budget follows converting segments while the female segment grows.
- **CRM case study — 10th Planet Portland: +288% (2016–2024) from lead follow-up automation**, win-back campaigns (40–60 reactivations per campaign), email segmentation (open rates 14%→54%). **Lead follow-up systems outperform ad spend.**
- Paid ads: useful but secondary — conservative budget, simple geo/age/interest Facebook campaigns, scale what works.

### Digital/info products — realistic numbers
- BJJ Fanatics: 50% rev share, titles $47–several hundred, daily-deal driven. Non-celebrity realistic: **$5K–50K+/course**, or **$2K–15K/mo** via subscription/Patreon. Ceiling is celebrity-driven (Gordon Ryan est. $500K–1M+/yr).
- Subscription curricula: Henry Akins model; Gracie University = category leader.
- 🔑 **The highest income in BJJ is built on teaching infrastructure, not prize money** — but for a local owner, digital is a **long-game brand play**. What pays sooner: branded apparel, seminars, camps, private packs.

### Local competitive set (Dripping Springs)
- **Stoic Jiu Jitsu** — adult/teen/kids programs + summer camps + free trials.
- **Jean Jacques Machado affiliate** — Machado lineage school in Dripping Springs.
- Austin's elite adult market is 30 min away (Kingsway, B Team, etc.).

### 6A. Joao's stated priorities — alignment check (added 2026-06-11)
Joao's three asks, confirmed directly: **(1) more privates, (2) his school/curriculum system (Line B), (3) more group students, especially kids.** The plan covers all three, with two adjustments from first-party class observations (kids ~15–17/class vs. adults 2–3/class):

- **Privates — pull forward from Phase 2.** No new infrastructure needed: a privates section + CTA goes on the landing page v1, and the near-empty adult time slots are exactly the mat hours privates can fill. Highest margin, his stated want, supply already exists.
- **Adult program has a cold-start problem — don't market into it as-is.** Inviting adults to a 2-person class triggers the social-proof death spiral (visitors see emptiness and don't join, keeping it empty). - **Kids — the engine is already proven** (15–17/class is real critical mass). Growth = referral mechanics (Buddy Week works best where mass exists), capacity math (intake 6.2: which slots can absorb more vs. need a new slot), and the landing-page funnel.
